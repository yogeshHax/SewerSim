# SewerSim

### Intelligent Sewer Network Simulation & Failure Detection

> **Turning underground water infrastructure into an interactive digital twin — so failures can be simulated before they become real-world problems.**

**Theme:** Water Resilience

---

## 🌊 What is SewerSim?

**SewerSim** is an intelligent 3D digital-twin platform for understanding, simulating, and analyzing sewer infrastructure.

Urban sewer networks are critical to water resilience, but they are difficult to inspect and understand because much of the infrastructure exists underground. During extreme rainfall, flooding, blockages, leaks, pump failures, and capacity overloads can cause wastewater to overflow or enter surrounding environments, creating risks for water quality and urban infrastructure.

SewerSim transforms a sewer network from a **static infrastructure map** into an **interactive simulation environment**.

It combines:

**CAD / Infrastructure Data → Network Graph → 3D Digital Twin → Hydraulic Simulation → AI Analysis → Failure Detection → What-if Testing**

---

# 🚨 The Problem

A sewer network is not simply a collection of pipes.

It is a connected infrastructure system involving:

* Pipes
* Junctions
* Manholes
* Pumps
* Outlets
* Elevations
* Flow paths
* Surface conditions
* Rainfall and inflow
* Hydraulic capacity

When one component fails, the consequences can propagate through the network.

For example:

```text
Extreme Rainfall
       ↓
Increased Inflow
       ↓
Network Capacity Exceeded
       ↓
Water Level Rises
       ↓
Overflow / Hydraulic Failure
       ↓
Flooding & Wastewater Exposure
       ↓
Potential Water-Quality Risk
```

Traditional infrastructure maps can tell us **where a pipe exists**.

They do not necessarily tell us:

> **What happens if this pipe becomes blocked during extreme rainfall?**

That is the problem SewerSim attempts to address.

---

# 💧 Why SewerSim is a Water Resilience Project

Although the project focuses on sewer infrastructure, its broader objective is **water resilience**.

Water resilience is not only about supplying freshwater. It also involves protecting water resources and maintaining infrastructure under extreme conditions.

Flooding and sewer failures can create pathways through which untreated wastewater reaches the surrounding environment and potentially affects groundwater or surface-water resources.

Therefore:

> **SewerSim focuses on making wastewater infrastructure more resilient in order to reduce risks to the wider urban water system.**

The project does **not** assume that wastewater automatically contaminates groundwater or freshwater pipelines.

Instead, it models the **physical conditions and failure pathways** under which such contamination risks could occur.

---

# 🔬 Case Study Approach

SewerSim is designed to demonstrate its capabilities using a **real documented infrastructure/flooding incident** rather than inventing a fictional disaster.

The case-study methodology follows:

```text
Real-world incident
        ↓
Documented environmental / infrastructure evidence
        ↓
Available network & geographic information
        ↓
Network reconstruction
        ↓
SewerSim simulation
        ↓
Comparable failure scenario
        ↓
Risk / vulnerability identification
        ↓
Potential intervention
        ↓
Before vs After simulation
```

### Evidence is separated into three categories

#### 🟢 Documented

Information supported by external evidence:

* Rainfall
* Flooding
* Reported sewage overflow
* Groundwater measurements
* Water-quality measurements
* Wastewater generation
* Incident timeline

#### 🔵 Reconstructed

Information derived from available infrastructure/geospatial data:

* Network topology
* Pipe locations
* Junctions
* Manholes
* Elevations
* Catchment characteristics

#### 🟠 Simulated

Values generated by SewerSim:

* Flow
* Pressure
* Water depth
* Capacity utilization
* Overflow
* Failure propagation
* Hypothetical contamination pathways

This distinction prevents simulated results from being presented as historical measurements.

# 🧠 How SewerSim Works

## 1. Infrastructure Input

The intended workflow begins with infrastructure information such as:

* CAD files
* GIS data
* Network data
* Engineering information

The system identifies infrastructure components and converts them into a structured representation.

```text
CAD / GIS / Network Data
          ↓
Component Extraction
          ↓
Network Graph
```

---

## 2. Network Graph

The infrastructure is represented as a graph.

```text
Nodes = infrastructure components

Edges = physical connections / pipes
```

Conceptually:

```text
          J2
         /  \
       J1    J4 ─── OUT
       |      |
     SOURCE   J3
```

Every pipe has:

* Source node
* Destination node
* Diameter
* Geometry
* Elevation information

This allows the system to reason about connectivity rather than treating the 3D model as arbitrary geometry.

---

## 3. Topology Validation

Before visualization or simulation, the network can be checked for structural problems.

Examples include:

* Invalid pipe endpoints
* Disconnected components
* Duplicate IDs
* Missing nodes
* Incorrect connections
* Invalid geometry
* Unreachable destinations
* Pipe intersections without actual junctions

This is important because:

> **A visually convincing 3D network is not necessarily a valid infrastructure network.**

SewerSim therefore separates **network logic** from **3D rendering**.

Possible scenarios include:

### Blockage

```text
Pipe blockage
      ↓
Reduced capacity
      ↓
Upstream water level increases
      ↓
```

### Extreme Rainfall

```text
Heavy rainfall
      ↓
Increased inflow
      ↓
Hydraulic loading
      ↓
```

```text

      ↓
```

# 🤖 AI Analysis Layer

AI is not intended to replace the hydraulic simulation.

Instead, the two layers have different responsibilities.

### Physics / Hydraulic Engine

Answers:

> **What is happening physically?**

Examples:

* Flow increased
* Pressure increased
* Water level exceeded capacity
* A section became overloaded
* Overflow occurred

### AI Layer

Answers:

> **What does it mean?**

The AI can analyze:

* Network topology
* Simulation state
* Anomalies
* High-risk locations
* Possible causes
* Failure propagation
* Scenario differences

It can also provide natural-language explanations and assist with **what-if analysis**.

For example:

> **"Why is this section at risk?"**

The system can examine the network and simulation state and explain the contributing factors.

# 🔍 Failure Detection

SewerSim is designed to identify infrastructure conditions such as:

* Blockages
* Leaks
* Hydraulic overload
* Abnormal pressure
* Excessive water depth
* Pump failure
* Overflow
* Vulnerable network sections

A failure can then be visualized directly in the 3D environment.

Instead of simply displaying:

> `Pipe J-014 → J-015 : HIGH RISK`

the system can connect the analysis back to the actual infrastructure location.

# 🧪 What-if Analysis

One of the central ideas behind SewerSim is:

> **Don't wait for infrastructure to fail in the real world. Test the failure virtually first.**

A user can create a scenario such as:

```text
"What happens if this pipe becomes blocked?"
```

or:

```text
"What happens during extreme rainfall?"
```

The system can then compare the network state before and after the scenario.

### Example

```text
BASELINE
↓
Normal flow

        ↓

FAILURE SCENARIO
↓
Blockage introduced
↓
Hydraulic stress increases
↓
Overflow risk detected

        ↓

INTERVENTION
↓
Modify / remove / improve infrastructure

        ↓

RE-SIMULATE
↓
Compare results
```

This transforms SewerSim from a visualization tool into a **decision-support environment**.

# 🧱 Architecture Philosophy

SewerSim intentionally separates the major responsibilities of the system.

```text
DATA
 ↓
NETWORK LOGIC
 ↓
PHYSICS
 ↓
VISUALIZATION
 ↓
AI
```

This prevents the 3D model from becoming the source of truth.

The **network graph is the logical source of truth**.

The 3D model is a visualization of that network.

The simulation operates on the infrastructure model.

The AI interprets simulation and network data.

---

# 📊 Validation Philosophy

SewerSim distinguishes between:

### Historical validation

Comparing the simulation with known observations from a documented event.

### Scenario simulation

Testing a hypothetical failure under defined conditions.

### Prediction

Estimating how the network could behave under future conditions.

These should never be presented as the same thing.

For example:

> **Historical fact:** An area experienced sewage overflow.

> **Simulation:** SewerSim reproduces comparable hydraulic conditions.

> **Prediction:** Under the modeled conditions, this network section could experience overflow.

This distinction is fundamental to the project's methodology.

# ⚠️ Current Limitations

SewerSim is a research/prototype project and does not claim to replace professional municipal hydraulic modelling or engineering systems.

Current limitations may include:

* Incomplete public infrastructure data
* Estimated parameters where engineering data is unavailable
* Prototype CAD-processing workflows
* Simplified environmental assumptions
* Limited groundwater modelling
* Limited contaminant-transport modelling
* Scenario-based rather than universally predictive AI analysis

If a parameter is estimated, it should be explicitly identified as an **assumption or reconstruction**, rather than presented as a measured real-world value.

# 🌍 Impact

SewerSim aims to help shift infrastructure management from:

> **Reacting after failure**

to:

> **Understanding and testing failure before it happens.**

By combining digital twins, hydraulic simulation, 3D visualization and AI-assisted analysis, SewerSim provides a way to investigate how underground water infrastructure behaves under stress.

The ultimate goal is not simply to visualize pipes.

It is to help answer:

> **Where can the system fail?**

> **Why can it fail?**

> **What happens if it fails?**

> **What can we change before it becomes a real-world problem?**

# 🔬 Research Principle

SewerSim follows a simple principle:

> **Never confuse what is known, what is reconstructed, and what is simulated.**

Real-world infrastructure data should remain distinguishable from assumptions and model outputs.

This makes the system more useful not only as a visualization platform, but as a framework for **transparent infrastructure experimentation**.
